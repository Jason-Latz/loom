It is also the atlas's first demonstration of its own method: here is an object, here is how the date was extracted from it, here is the error bar, here is the argument still running, here is what changed when people looked again. If the reader finishes this lesson believing that human origins are a *question with evidence* rather than a *fact with a date*, the node has done its work and the rest of Era I is legible. Do not let it become a fossil-of-the-week.

## 2. Preferred scene

**A hearth at Jebel Irhoud, in the deposit archaeologists now call layer 7, around 315,000 years ago. Someone is working flint beside a fire, and flakes are falling into the embers.**

This scene is chosen because the fire is simultaneously the subject and the instrument. The site preserves "several in situ fire features" (MPI-EVA field-project page). The flints that fell into those fires were heated, and heating is what reset their luminescence clock; three hundred thousand years of background radiation refilled it, and in 2017 Daniel Richter's team read that accumulated dose back out as an age (Richter et al., *Nature* 546, 293-296). **The fire that fed these people is the fire that dated them.** Nothing else available to this node fuses the human scene and the epistemics that tightly.

**Documentation ledger.**

- **Attested by excavation.** In situ fire features at the site (MPI-EVA). Heated flint artefacts, thermoluminescence-dated, weighted average 315 ± 34 ka (Richter et al. 2017, abstract). A Middle Stone Age assemblage of Levallois prepared cores and points, with handaxes absent, and most tools made on high-quality flint imported to the site (MPG press release, 2017). Hunted fauna dominated by gazelle, with hartebeest, wildebeest, zebra, buffalo, porcupine, hare, tortoise, ostrich eggshell, freshwater molluscs, leopard and hyena also present, and bones broken for marrow (sci.news and Popular Archaeology, both secondary; primary is Amani & Geraads 1993 and Geraads et al. 2013, neither of which I could open). Layer 7 is a single bone bed in the lower part of the deposits holding "the remains of at least five individuals (three adults, one adolescent and one immature individual, around 7.5 years old)" (Hublin et al. 2017, p. 290, read in full). The site sits in the Pleistocene fill of a karstic network in the Jebel Irhoud massif, "55 km south-east of Safi" (Hublin et al. 2017, p. 289). The anatomy: face and mandible plot inside recent-modern-human variation; the braincase and endocast do not, being elongated and not globular (Hublin et al. 2017, Fig. 3).
- **Reconstruction, and must be labelled as such in `storyContext`.** Every human being in the scene. Names, ages beyond the child's ~7.5 years, sex, kinship, who taught whom, dialogue, speech of any kind, weather, season, time of day, whether the five people in layer 7 knew each other, whether anyone at Irhoud had language, and any interior life whatsoever.
- **Real named person available: no.** None exists, at any date in the Pleistocene, anywhere in this node. The only real names belong to the twentieth and twenty-first centuries: Émile Ennouchi, Jean-Jacques Hublin, Abdelouahed Ben-Ncer, Daniel Richter, Teresa Steele, Shannon McPherron, Philipp Gunz. Use them in the coda and in significance, never in the Pleistocene scene.
- **Quantities to check before printing.** Do **not** print a kilometre figure for the imported flint: every source I opened says "imported" and none gives a distance (UNVERIFIED). Do **not** print a count of thermoluminescence-dated flints: a search snippet says fourteen, I could not open the paper to confirm it (UNVERIFIED). Do **not** print "472 identified animal bones" without the primary faunal report (secondary only). Do **not** print an age of 7.8 years for the child; Hublin et al. say "around 7.5 years old" (UNVERIFIED at 7.8). Do **not** claim a hearth was inside a cave chamber with a described shape; the cave was largely destroyed by mining and the geometry of the original chamber is not something I could verify.

**Why this scene works.** It dramatizes the node's actual mechanism twice over. The prepared core is a plan held in a head before it exists in stone: you shape the block so that one struck flake comes off already finished. That is craft as forethought, and it was being done in Morocco and in Kenya at the same time. And the fire is the thread that ties the scene to the reader, because the only reason anyone knows any of this is that some flakes fell into it.

### Alternate 1 (recommended as the story's final paragraph, not the opening)

**The barite mine, 1960 to 1962.** Mining operations in the Jebel Irhoud massif exposed a Palaeolithic site in 1960; an almost complete skull, Irhoud 1, "was accidentally unearthed in 1961, prompting excavations" (Hublin et al. 2017, p. 289). Émile Ennouchi published it in 1962 under the title *Un Néanderthalien: l'homme du Jebel Irhoud (Maroc)* (*Anthropologie* 66, 279-299; verified from the reference list of Hublin et al. 2017, ref. 9). The remains "were initially considered to be around 40 thousand years old and an African form of Neanderthals" (Hublin et al. 2017, p. 289). The pathos is enormous and entirely documented: the oldest known face of our species sat in a museum in Rabat for half a century under somebody else's name and off by a factor of eight in date. **Use this as the story's closing paragraph.** Ending on the words *un Néanderthalien* is the best pivot available to this lesson: the reader arrives at significance already wanting to know how a skull gets its species back.

Do not embellish the discovery. Tertiary accounts (Wikipedia, Grokipedia, Popular Archaeology) add a mine doctor, two thousand tons of debris cleared with explosives, a 1961 Ennouchi expedition, and 1967-1969 work by Jacques Tixier and Roger de Bayle des Hermens. I could not verify any of that against a scholarly source. The verified spine is: mining exposed the site in 1960, the skull came out in 1961, Ennouchi called it a Neanderthal in 1962.

### Alternate 2 (safest, zero composite characters)

**Open in the luminescence lab.** A flake of flint that has not seen light since it left the fire is ground in a darkroom, heated, and made to glow; the brightness of that glow is a measure of how much radiation the crystal has absorbed since the last time it was hot. Everything in the scene is attested, no person is invented, and the lesson's governing image arrives in the first sentence. Weaker on human pressure, and it risks opening the whole atlas on a laboratory instead of a person, which is the wrong first impression for a 365-day reading habit. Hold in reserve only if the composite feels thin.

### Scene to AVOID

**The birth of the first human being.** A mother, a first baby, the first *Homo sapiens* opening its eyes. This is the most seductive scene available and it is the precise thing the node exists to destroy: there was no first one, no threshold crossed on a particular afternoon, and every specialist quoted in this brief is arguing about where a line could even be drawn. Writing that scene would sell the reader the exact story the lesson then has to spend five paragraphs taking back.

**Also avoid as a frame:** anyone at Irhoud looking north or east toward the lands their descendants will settle. Even at the fast end of the evidence, the out-of-Africa dispersals are roughly two hundred and fifty thousand years downstream of this fire. Nothing at Jebel Irhoud is a departure.

## 3. Story movement

Six paragraphs. Five in deep time, one coda in 1961.

1. **The fire and the flake.** Cold open on the knapping: the imported flint, the prepared core, the point coming off, a spent flake nudged into the embers and forgotten. Sensory and specific. No preamble, no "picture a world."
2. **The hunt and the meal.** Gazelle, mostly. Marrow cracked out of long bones. Ostrich eggshell, tortoise, freshwater shells. Teresa Steele's finding, that small game is a small fraction and these people were serious hunters, is worth dramatizing rather than stating. This is where the reader learns these are competent adults running a landscape, not almost-people.
3. **The child.** The immature individual of layer 7, around seven and a half. The load-bearing fact here is not cuteness: the child's teeth were growing on a schedule closer to a modern human's than to any earlier member of *Homo* (Smith et al. 2007, PNAS). A long childhood is a species-level commitment to teaching, and it is already in place. Handle this without stating that they taught language.
4. **The face across the fire.** What an observer would actually see: a face you could pass in a street, short, tucked back under the brow, and, above it, a skull long and low, running back much further than yours does. Two anatomies on one neck. Let the reader feel the mismatch as a physical fact before significance explains it.
5. **The pivot in deep time.** The fire goes out. The flake cools. The cave fills. State plainly what the reader now cannot help asking: if the face was already finished and the skull was not, what exactly was still being built, and for how long?
6. **Coda, 1961.** The hill is cut open for barite. A skull comes out of the wall. It is given a name, and the name is *un Néanderthalien*, and it will keep that name for fifty-five years. End there.

**Governing image: the fire as clock.** It lights the scene, it cooks the food, it is what the flake fell into, and it is the only reason there is a date at all. Bring it back once at the close of significance, changed: a fire that has been out for three hundred thousand years is still the most precise instrument in the room.

**Words and framings to avoid.** "Mysterious." "Vanished." "Primitive" applied to people (fine applied to a named anatomical character, as the literature does). "Missing link." "Dawn of man." "Cradle of humankind" as a real place (name it only to dismiss it). "Not yet fully human" and every variant of almost-us. "Our ancestors finally became us." Any sentence in which Africa is a starting line and somewhere else is the race. Any implication that these people were on the way to anywhere. Do not call them Moroccans or North Africans in a national sense. Do not use "Eden" more than once, and only as the thing being refused. And do not let "300,000 years ago" appear without its error bar the first time it is stated in significance.

## 4. Significance spine

Five movements. The threads the lesson must name in words are **craft** and **ideas** (the node carries exactly those two pigments, correctly). Craft: the prepared core, controlled fire, the imported flint, and, in the modern half, thermoluminescence as a craft of reading. Ideas: what a species is, who gets to be inside it, and how a boundary drawn on a page turns out to depend on gaps in a record. Say both out loud; do not gesture.

1. **How we know, and how much the answer has moved.** Thermoluminescence on fire-heated flints gives a weighted average of **315 ± 34 thousand years**, i.e. roughly 281 to 349 ka; a recalculated uranium-series/electron-spin-resonance date on a tooth from the Irhoud 3 mandible gives **286 ± 32 ka**, and both are consistent with the faunal and microfaunal assemblages (Richter et al. 2017, abstract, verified). Set that against the site's own history: about 40,000 years in the 1960s; 160 ± 16 ka from a direct date on the same jaw in 2007 (Smith et al., PNAS 104, 6128-6133); 315 ± 34 ka in 2017. The number has moved by nearly a factor of eight inside one human lifetime. This is the atlas's first and best chance to teach a reader that a date is a measurement with a method attached, not a fact handed down.

2. **The mosaic: why the face was finished and the skull was not.** The face, mandible and dentition align with early and recent anatomically modern humans; the neurocranium and endocast do not, being elongated rather than globular (Hublin et al. 2017). Size was not the issue: the endocranial volumes of Irhoud 1 and Irhoud 2 are **1,375 ml and 1,467 ml**, inside present-day human range (Neubauer, Hublin & Gunz, *Science Advances* 4(1), 2018, Table 1, verified). Shape was. That same paper finds brain shape reaching present-day human variation only **between about 100,000 and 35,000 years ago**. So a modern face was in place roughly two hundred thousand years before a modern-shaped brain. This is the mechanism that makes the `spark-of-mind` edge honest, and it is the reason the node's summary says "the face first and the rounded braincase much later."

3. **Why there, and what pan-African actually means.** It does not mean humans came from Morocco. Jebel Irhoud is the northwest corner of a continent-scale process, and the evidence for that is contemporaneity at enormous distances. At Olorgesailie in southern Kenya, roughly six thousand kilometres away (my great-circle arithmetic from approximate coordinates; treat as "roughly six thousand"), Middle Stone Age assemblages with prepared cores and points date to about **320,000 to 295,000 years ago**, with stone materials procured from **25 to 50 km** away and iron-rich rocks worked for red pigment (Brooks et al., *Science* 360, 90-94, 2018; chronology in Deino et al., *Science* 360, 95-98, 2018). Florisbad in South Africa, tentatively about 260 ka, is read as a primitive *H. sapiens* (Grün et al. 1996, cited in Hublin et al. 2017). Same technological horizon, one continent, no single garden required. Say the size of the thing: Africa is not a country and the distances here are Atlantic-scale. This is the movement that must carry Hublin's line about dispersal *within* Africa, and it is the setup the `great-dispersal` edge needs.

4. **The named live debate: is Jebel Irhoud us?** Use this one; it is concrete, it has named people on both sides, and it does not duplicate `spark-of-mind`. Hublin's team claims the fossils document "early stages of the *H. sapiens* clade in which key features of modern morphology were established" and that the process "involved the whole African continent." **John Hawks** (University of Wisconsin-Madison) counters that "maybe Jebel Irhoud was evolving into modern humans, but another possibility is that it is retaining facial morphology from an *H. antecessor*-like population," and notes the team did not compare the remains with the roughly 800,000-year-old Spanish *H. antecessor* material. **Marta Mirazón Lahr** (Cambridge) puts the dilemma as a fork: if a globular braincase is what defines the species, then Irhoud are close cousins rather than ancestors; if the face defines it, then the discovery just moves the pin from sub-Saharan Africa to the Mediterranean, which is the same mistake in a new place. Her own modelling with Aurélien Mounier (*Nature Communications* 10, 3406, 2019) concludes that *H. sapiens* "appears to have originated from the coalescence of South and, possibly, East-African source populations, while North-African fossils may represent a population which introgressed into Neandertals." No verdict. And note the concession inside the Hublin paper itself: "Delimiting clear-cut anatomical boundaries for a 'modern' grade within the *H. sapiens* clade thus only depends on gaps in the fossil record." A second debate is available if wanted, between the structured-metapopulation model (Scerri et al. 2018, twenty-three authors: interlinked groups across Africa whose connectivity changed with ecological boundaries) and the weakly-structured-stem model (Ragsdale et al., *Nature* 617, 755-763, 2023: two or more weakly differentiated ancestral populations connected by gene flow over hundreds of thousands of years, with the earliest divergence among living populations only 120,000 to 135,000 years ago). Both refuse the garden; they disagree about how deep and how hard the internal walls were. **Do not run more than one debate at length.**

5. **Africa at this date was crowded, and the modern world still carries the argument.** Do the arithmetic on the page. Jebel Irhoud, 315 ± 34 ka, is 281 to 349. The Kabwe (Broken Hill) skull from Zambia, long thought half a million years old, was redated in 2020 to **299 ± 25 ka**, i.e. 274 to 324, and is *H. heidelbergensis/rhodesiensis* (Grün et al., *Nature* 580, 372-375). The *Homo naledi* deposit in the Rising Star cave in South Africa dates to **335 to 236 ka** (Dirks et al., *eLife* 6:e24231, 2017). Three different human anatomies, three corners of one continent, overlapping ranges. Grün's team draws the conclusion explicitly: later Middle Pleistocene Africa "contained multiple contemporaneous hominin lineages." That is the leg the `the-other-humans` edge is missing, and it is the honest end of the Eden story: not one origin point, not one kind of human, not one line. Land the last paragraph on the ideas thread. Every popular version of "when we became human" is a claim about where to draw a line, and this record keeps declining to supply one; the boundary we assume is inherited from gaps in a museum drawer, and the drawer keeps being reopened. Close on the fire: an object cools, and three hundred millennia later the cooling is the measurement.

## 5. Factual traps

1. **Trap:** Jebel Irhoud is the oldest *Homo sapiens*, full stop. **Truth:** the careful formulations are the published ones. Hublin et al. call the site "the oldest and richest African Middle Stone Age hominin site that documents early stages of the *H. sapiens* clade"; Richter et al. say the human remains "are the oldest reported for *H. sapiens*." Whether the fossils belong to our species at all is actively disputed by Hawks and Mirazón Lahr. Write "the oldest fossils widely attributed to our species," not "the first humans." **Source:** *Nature* 546, 289-292 and 546, 293-296 (abstracts verified via PubMed 28593953 and 28593967); *Scientific American*, "Ancient Fossils from Morocco Mess Up Modern Human Origins."

2. **Trap:** the skull was found in 1960. **Truth:** mining operations exposed the site in 1960; Irhoud 1 "was accidentally unearthed in 1961, prompting excavations." Popular sources collapse the two. **Source:** Hublin et al. 2017, *Nature* 546, p. 289, read in full from the author-institute PDF.

3. **Trap:** Irhoud 1 was recognized as an early modern human from the start. **Truth:** Émile Ennouchi published it in 1962 as *Un Néanderthalien: l'homme du Jebel Irhoud (Maroc)*, the deposits were thought to be about 40,000 years old, and as recently as 2007 a direct date on the Irhoud 3 tooth gave 160 ± 16 ka. **Source:** Ennouchi 1962, *Anthropologie* 66, 279-299 (verified from Hublin et al. 2017, ref. 9); Smith et al. 2007, *PNAS* 104(15), 6128-6133 (abstract verified, PubMed 17372199); Hublin et al. 2017, p. 289.

4. **Trap:** their brains were smaller, or less developed. **Truth:** endocranial volume was already inside the modern range. Irhoud 1 is 1,375 ml and Irhoud 2 is 1,467 ml. What differs is shape: the Irhoud braincases "are not globular," and are intermediate between *H. erectus* and Neanderthals on the shape analysis. **Source:** Neubauer, Hublin & Gunz, "The evolution of modern human brain shape," *Science Advances* 4(1), 2018, eaao5961, Table 1 (open access, verified at PMC5783678).

5. **Trap:** the site is 315,000 years old. **Truth:** 315 ± 34 thousand years is a weighted average of thermoluminescence ages, spanning roughly 281 to 349 ka, supported by a recalculated U-series/ESR age of 286 ± 32 ka on an Irhoud 3 tooth. Print the uncertainty at least once. **Source:** Richter et al. 2017, abstract (PubMed 28593967).

6. **Trap:** the discovery pushed human origins back a hundred thousand years, and still does. **Truth:** the "hundred thousand" compares 315 ka against the then-standard ~195 ka age for Omo Kibish, so the 2017 figure was about 120,000 years. But Omo Kibish has since been redated to a **minimum of 233 ± 22 ka**, so the gap has partly closed from the other side: 315 minus 233 is about 82,000 years, and the error bars overlap. The node's hook is a 2017-vintage sentence. **Source:** Vidal et al., "Age of the oldest known *Homo sapiens* from eastern Africa," *Nature* 601, 579-583, 2022 (abstract verified, PubMed 35022610); Richter et al. 2017 abstract for the 195 kyr figure.

7. **Trap:** these people were alone, the only humans in Africa. **Truth:** the Kabwe skull is 299 ± 25 ka and belongs to *H. heidelbergensis/rhodesiensis*; the *Homo naledi* deposit is 335 to 236 ka. Grün's team concludes that later Middle Pleistocene Africa contained multiple contemporaneous hominin lineages, mirroring Eurasia. **Source:** Grün et al., *Nature* 580, 372-375, 2020 (PubMed 32296179); Dirks et al., *eLife* 6:e24231, 2017 (PubMed 28483040).

8. **Trap:** the five people of layer 7 were a family, or were buried. **Truth:** layer 7 is a bone bed containing "the remains of at least five individuals (three adults, one adolescent and one immature individual, around 7.5 years old)," accumulated "in a rather constrained window of time." No burial is claimed, no kinship is claimed, no simultaneous death is claimed. **Source:** Hublin et al. 2017, *Nature* 546, p. 290.

9. **Trap:** shared Middle Stone Age tools mean a continental network of contact or trade. **Truth:** the argument is a shared technological horizon appearing across Africa in the same window, plus local raw-material transport measured in tens of kilometres (25 to 50 km at Olorgesailie). The Irhoud flint is described as imported, but I found **no published distance** for it. Do not print one, and do not upgrade "widespread technique" into "network." **Source:** Richter et al. 2017 (potentially pan-African origin for species and industry); Brooks et al., *Science* 360, 90-94, 2018.

10. **Trap:** put a handaxe in the scene. **Truth:** the Irhoud assemblage is Levallois prepared-core technology with pointed forms most common, and handaxes, typical of older sites, are absent. **Source:** MPG press release, "The first of our kind," 2017 (fetched and verified).

11. **Trap:** this proves humans originated in Morocco, or that the cradle has moved from East Africa to North Africa. **Truth:** the paper's claim is the opposite, that the process "involved the whole African continent," and Mirazón Lahr's published warning is specifically that reading Irhoud as ancestral would simply relocate the pin from sub-Saharan Africa to the Mediterranean. **Source:** Hublin et al. 2017, abstract; *Scientific American* 2017.

12. **Trap:** they made art, wore ornaments, or spoke. **Truth:** nothing at Jebel Irhoud attests symbolic behaviour. Fire, Levallois points, hunted gazelle, marrow. Red pigment does appear in the contemporary Kenyan record at Olorgesailie, but that is iron-rich rock procurement at a different site and is not evidence about Irhoud. Ochre kits, beads and engraving belong to `spark-of-mind`, roughly two hundred thousand years later. Leaving this hole open is a structural requirement of the `spark-of-mind` edge, not an omission. **Source:** absence in Hublin et al. 2017 and Richter et al. 2017; Brooks et al. 2018 for Olorgesailie pigment.

13. **Trap:** the site is 100 km west of Marrakesh. **Truth:** probably fine, and the MPI-EVA field-project page says exactly that, but the peer-reviewed paper locates the massif "55 km south-east of Safi." Prefer the paper's formulation; if you want Marrakesh for reader orientation, give it as "roughly a hundred kilometres" and attribute loosely. My own great-circle check on approximate coordinates gave 61 km to Safi and 85 km to Marrakesh, which is close enough to both to be useless as a correction. **Source:** Hublin et al. 2017, p. 289; MPI-EVA field-project page.

14. **Trap:** the Sahara was green at this moment, and that is why people were there. **Truth:** the Sahara has swung between savannah and desert well over two hundred times in the last eight million years, driven by orbital forcing, and those green periods are real and consequential. But I could **not** verify a documented humid phase at 315 ka specifically. Say the Sahara oscillates; do not pin this fire to a named green period. The site's own fauna (gazelle, hartebeest, wildebeest, zebra, ostrich, tortoise, freshwater molluscs) is the safe local evidence for open, watered grassland. **Source:** Larrasoaña, Roberts & Rohling, "Dynamics of Green Sahara Periods and Their Role in Hominin Evolution," *PLOS ONE* 8(10), e76514, 2013 (verified; it does not identify an MIS 9 green period in what I could read). UNVERIFIED for 315 ka.

## 6. Callback design

**There are no legal callback targets. This node is `globalIndex: 0`.** The packet lists `earlierWrittenLessons: []` and `earlierUnwrittenPeers: []`, and the question mix is `recall 1, why 4, callback 0`. Do not write a `callback` question and do not set `callbackTo` on anything. The forge spec's two-callback default is explicitly suspended for very early nodes, and here it is suspended completely.

What replaces it is a designed sequence of five. Suggested shape, as one learning arc rather than five checks:

1. **Recall.** The load-bearing fact is the mosaic, not the date. Ask what the Jebel Irhoud fossils actually look like and why that combination was a surprise. The answer teaches: modern face and jaw, archaic elongated braincase, brain already modern in volume (1,375 and 1,467 ml) but not in shape.
2. **Why (method).** How does a stone tool that fell into a fire tell you its age, and why did the same site's date move from 40,000 to 160,000 to 315,000 years within sixty years? This is the atlas's first epistemics lesson; spend the answer on it.
3. **Why (mechanism).** Why does a single origin point become unnecessary once you have contemporaneous Middle Stone Age sites six thousand kilometres apart? What would have to be true for the garden model to survive?
4. **Why (counterfactual).** If a globular braincase is what defines *Homo sapiens*, what happens to the Jebel Irhoud people, and if a modern face is what defines it, what happens instead? Force the reader to feel that the species boundary is a decision, not a discovery. This is where Hawks and Mirazón Lahr earn their place.
5. **Why (forward-loading).** Bodies like ours existed for roughly two hundred thousand years before ochre kits and beads appear. What does that gap rule out, and what does it not rule out? **Do not answer it.** This question is the handshake with `spark-of-mind`, which already asks a version of it as its own fourth question and already carries the two-camp explanation. Set the puzzle; let Blombos pay it off.

**Do not restate spark-of-mind's debate.** That lesson (already written, `data/lessons/spark-of-mind.js`, third significance paragraph) explicitly names Jebel Irhoud and runs the late-neurological-upgrade versus slow-cultural-accumulation argument at length. If `first-of-our-kind` runs the same debate, the atlas repeats itself on days one and nine. Use the "is Irhoud us?" debate instead. Also note `world-the-ice-made` twice uses "roughly 300,000 years" as the span of anatomically modern humanity; this lesson is the node that licenses that figure, so keep the number compatible and let it be the one place the error bar is shown.

**Callbacks this lesson must earn from later nodes.** Three later lessons will reach back here, so plant the hooks cleanly: `spark-of-mind` needs the body-without-the-symbols; `great-dispersal` needs continental range and habitat breadth inside Africa; `the-other-humans` needs Kabwe and *naledi* so its Eurasian cast is not the whole story. Write each of those three facts as a sentence a later writer can quote.

It is earned twice, because Jebel Irhoud has in situ fire features and because fire is literally the instrument that produced the date. See Node-data flag 7: this may be worth a real graph edge.

## 8. Node-data flags

Precise problems in the committed graph data. **Do not edit any repo file.** Reported for Codex.

1. **Hook: "push the origin of our species back a hundred thousand years" is 2017-vintage arithmetic and has partly expired.** In 2017 the comparison was 315 ka against a ~195 ka Omo Kibish, i.e. about 120,000 years, which press coverage rounded variously to 100,000 and 115,000. Omo Kibish was redated in 2022 to a minimum of 233 ± 22 ka, so the current gap is about 82,000 years, with overlapping uncertainty. Suggest "pushes the fossil record of our species back by roughly a hundred thousand years" (true of what the discovery did, in 2017) or dropping the number.

2. **Hook: "a modern face on a long, archaic skull" is exactly right and should not be touched.** It is the paper's own finding in nine words. Flagging it here so it does not get churned in a later pass.

3. **Summary: "about 315,000 years old" drops the error bar.** Acceptable in a 60-word summary. The lesson must carry ± 34 ka, and should also carry the corroborating 286 ± 32 ka on the Irhoud 3 tooth.

4. **Summary: "the face first and the rounded braincase much later" is well supported but unquantified.** The supporting figure is that present-day brain-shape variation is reached only between about 100,000 and 35,000 years ago (Neubauer et al. 2018), so "much later" means roughly two hundred thousand years later. The lesson should supply that number so the phrase is not read as vague.

5. **Summary: "The Middle Stone Age tools beside them were already shared far and wide" risks implying a network.** What the evidence supports is a shared technique appearing across the continent in the same window (Olorgesailie at 320 to 295 ka) plus raw-material transport of tens of kilometres at that site. The Irhoud flint is "imported" with no distance published in anything I could open. Suggest "the same prepared-core technique was already in use thousands of kilometres away." Not an error as written, but one bad sentence away from one.

6. **Summary: "did not switch on suddenly in one East African garden" is the right claim, and should not harden into "the pan-African model is settled."** Ragsdale et al. 2023 infer a weakly structured stem rather than deep subdivision, and Mounier and Mirazón Lahr 2019 argue North African fossils may sit off the direct line entirely. The summary is safe; the lesson must not overclaim past it.

7. **Graph architecture: consider an edge `first-of-our-kind` → `the-tended-fire`.** `the-tended-fire` sits at `sort: -299000`, immediately after this node, so the edge is forward and legal. Jebel Irhoud has several in situ fire features, and thermoluminescence on fire-heated flint is the technique that produced the node's headline date. As it stands the atlas's first node does not touch its second. Codex's call; the lesson will carry `the-tended-fire` in `threadsOut` regardless.

8. **Era span, confirming the packet's open flag.** Era I is declared "c. 300,000 to 10,000 BCE" while this node's central estimate is 315 ka BP (about 313,000 BCE) and its stated uncertainty reaches 349 ka. Defensible under "c." Two clean options: widen the era span to "c. 315,000 to 10,000 BCE," or leave it and let the node's own date label of c. 300,000 BCE stand as a deliberate rounding. Recommend leaving it, since the lesson will print the real range and a widened span would then need justifying against the era blurb. Either way the lesson should not draw the reader's attention to the era header.

9. **Threads are correct as assigned.** `craft` and `ideas`, with `power` and `wealth` rightly absent: there is no economy and no politics recoverable from this site, and inventing either would be the first false note in the atlas.
