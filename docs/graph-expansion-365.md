# Loom 365: graph expansion audit dossier

Prepared for the adversarial review on the morning of 2026-07-19.

## 1. What this is

Loom is a parchment-chart atlas of world history where every node opens a ten-minute lesson and Jason reads one node a day. The goal of this expansion is a graph of exactly 365 nodes, a full year of daily reading. The atlas held 129 nodes; today the design pass produced 236 new seeds, one per remaining day, bringing the planned total to 365.

The expansion honors four priorities set by Jason: philosophy anchored to a readable primary text (the strongest emphasis, carried in each ideas node reading anchor); trade, money, and technology told mechanism first; underrepresented regions (Africa, the Americas, Oceania, Southeast Asia, Central Asia) given deliberate depth rather than garnish; and renaissance breadth across science, medicine, arts, music, literature, food, disease, and daily life. One hard exclusion holds throughout: no military spine, war appears only inside nodes whose real subject is a mechanism.

Each seed is a node object (id, title, date, sort, region, x, threads, hook, summary, edges) plus non shipped rationale, reading anchor, and an insertAfter anchor naming the existing node it follows. These are seeds, not written lessons: the prose comes later. This dossier is the map for tomorrow, one section per era, followed by the consolidated flag checklist and the closing statistics.

### Era 1: The Long Dawn (8 flags)

- [ ] BP vs BCE convention: this era (existing 8 nodes and these 12) labels deep-time dates 'BCE' when the underlying figures are years-before-present; the ~1,950-year gap is negligible above ~20,000 but becomes visible for the youngest new nodes (wolf-at-the-fire, into-the-last-continents, pots-before-the-farm). - [ ] first-of-our-kind: Jebel Irhoud is ~315,000 BP, slightly older than the era's stated opening 'c. 300,000 to 10,000 BCE'; the node deliberately anchors the era's front edge and its summary says 315,000 while the date label says 300,000. Acceptable under 'c.' but confirm the era span/blurb should not be widened.
- [ ] ash-across-asia: the framing that H. sapiens were 'pressing east' through India at ~74,000 BP leans on Jwalapuram, whose Middle Palaeolithic toolmakers are NOT confirmed as H. sapiens; the main southern-route sapiens dispersal is often dated later (~60,000 BP).
