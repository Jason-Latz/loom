# Fable Brief: song-economic-miracle (Era V)

## Graph target

- Node id: `song-economic-miracle`. Title: The Song Economic Miracle. Date: c. 1000 CE (sort 1000). Region
  easia, x 90. Threads: wealth, craft (name both in words, per the forge spec).
- Outgoing edges: `song-gifts-go-west` (Era VI, c. 1250), `treasure-fleets` (Era VI, 1405),
  `great-convergence` (Era X, 2001).
- Incoming edges the lesson inherits and must not contradict: `grand-canal-spine` (the spine along which
  markets, cities and iron towns thickened), `tang-golden-network` (Tang cosmopolitanism maturing into Song
  commerce), `diamond-sutra` (woodblock craft becoming the Song print economy of books, exam aids and paper
  money), `rice-and-the-yangtze` (early-ripening varieties including Champa rice as one part of an
  agricultural expansion, already correctly hedged), `salt-and-iron` (the monopoly quarrel refought, meaning
  Wang Anshi's New Policies of 1069 to 1076; that node is unwritten, so do not lean on it).

Central claim, restated honestly: for roughly a century and a half the Northern Song ran the largest, most
monetized, most printed and most heavily smelted economy on earth, and it is the first Chinese economy that
left enough numbers behind for historians to argue about. The lesson's real subject is that argument, not a
list of firsts. The node is anchored at c. 1000 CE, but the best documented material clusters between 1023
and 1092: give real dates, do not gesture at "around 1000."

## Preferred scene: Su Shi and the coal at Pengcheng, January 1079

Su Shi (1037 to 1101), the age's most famous writer, was prefect of Xuzhou (Pengcheng) in the late 1070s. In
the twelfth month of the first year of Yuanfeng, which falls in January 1079, coal was found southwest of the
prefecture. Su Shi wrote a poem, "Stone Coal," with a prose preface: there had been no coal at Pengcheng
before; north of Baituzhen they now smelt iron with it and make weapons said to pierce rhinoceros hide; the
blast goes zhenzhen; ten thousand men labor and a thousand supervise; the chestnut forests can breathe easy.

Everything the lesson needs is in that one text: a fuel crisis, a fossil substitute, a blast furnace at
scale, state demand for weapons, and a poet noticing that a forest has been reprieved. It is dated within a
year of the 1078 mining survey from which every famous Song iron figure descends.

### Documentation ledger

Attested: Su Shi's authorship; the poem's title, preface, stated date and place; the find southwest of
Xuzhou; smelting north of Baituzhen; iron worked into weapons; the poem's own sensory content (bellows,
tapping, labor scale, the reprieved chestnut woods). Donald Wagner (Historical Metallurgy 37.1, 2003) judges
that the poem reads like the work of someone who had actually seen a coal fuelled blast furnace, while
allowing that Su Shi may have worked from a vivid report.

Reconstruction, to be labeled in storyContext: Su Shi physically at the pit or furnace on any particular
day; weather, smells, dialogue, any smelter's name, any interior thought; any suggestion that the poem is a
report to the throne. Real named person available: yes, but he is the witness, not the subject. Quote at
most a short phrase of Wagner's translation, in quotation marks, translator credited.

### Alternate A: the Jiaozi Bureau at Chengdu, 1023 to 1024

Sichuan ran on iron cash, heavy and bad at distance, so Chengdu merchant houses had been issuing redeemable
paper notes (jiaozi) against deposits since around 1000; several failed and could not redeem. In 1023 the
court, on the proposal of the prefect of Yizhou (Chengdu), Xue Tian, founded a Jiaozi Bureau (jiaozi wu) and
took the issue over; the first government series followed in 1024. Attested: the office, the year, the
proposal, the private predecessors and their failures, the iron cash context. Not attested: what a 1024 note
looked like in the hand. No Northern Song jiaozi survives, and the earliest surviving Chinese paper money is
Ming, from 1375, so the world's first state paper money exists for us only as a description of a promise. A
composite Chengdu shopkeeper is acceptable if labeled. Verify Xue Tian and the 1023 versus 1024 split
against von Glahn first.

### Alternate B: Shen Kuo at Dream Brook, c. 1088

Shen Kuo (1031 to 1095), politically finished after the Yongle City disaster of 1082, writing the Mengxi
bitan at his Dream Brook estate, sets down the only account anyone has of Bi Sheng (see trap 5). Do the date
math aloud: Bi Sheng worked around 1045, when Shen Kuo was about fourteen, so Shen is a recorder writing
roughly forty years later, not a witness.

Recommendation: open with Su Shi, place Chengdu in the significance section, and use Shen Kuo as the story's
last movement, where the most famous Song invention turns out to survive in one paragraph by one man.

## Story movement (five to eight paragraphs)

1. Open in the poem's world in January 1079: the black stone, the city that burned damp firewood the winter
   before, the woman who could not sell hers. Su Shi gives you that image.
2. What the stone is for. North China had been running out of wood; coal goes into the furnace at Baituzhen
   and iron comes out, and the iron becomes weapons for a dynasty with a long, expensive northern frontier.
3. Widen to the empire around the furnace: grain moving north on the Sui canal grid, a capital where the
   walled wards and curfews are gone and shops open onto the street, night markets, fire towers manned
   against a city built of wood and people.
4. Pivot to the governing idea, the credible substitute. Coal stands in for wood, paper for iron cash, a
   printed primer for a teacher, a clerk's copy of an exam paper for a candidate's handwriting.
5. End on the question the reader cannot avoid: how much iron was there, and how would anyone know?

Let the substitute image return at the end, changed, on the jiaozi, whose substance is nothing but the swap.
This is a poem-and-furnace lesson, not another court lesson; the failure mode is a list of inventions.

## Significance spine

1. **Evidence, and how bad it is.** The 1078 mining survey preserved in the Song huiyao jigao records
   government receipts of iron in twenty-eight prefectures totaling about 3,300 tonnes. Robert Hartwell
   (Chicago dissertation, 1963; articles 1962 and 1966) reasoned from the administrative rules to a
   multiplier, added untaxed and illegal output, and reached about 125,000 English tons, 114,000 metric
   tonnes, roughly 1.2 kg per head per year. Wagner reexamined the two assumptions underneath it, found
   neither justified, and concluded that the order of magnitude is plausible and Hartwell's qualitative case
   stands, but no reliable calculation is possible. Name both men; this honesty earns the rest.
2. **Mechanism one, fuel and furnace.** Timber shortage in the north; coal in heating and industry; blast
   furnaces excavated in seven provinces; water power widely used for the blast. The best evidence in the
   lesson: three cast iron artefacts of Song and Yuan date radiocarbon to 11,540, 12,400 and 13,840 years
   before present, impossible in exactly the way that proves fossil carbon in the charge. Qiu Shihua and Cai
   Lianzhen calculate roughly three parts charcoal to seven parts coal; high sulphur in Song ironwork points
   the same way. Say coal, not coke.
3. **Mechanism two, money that is only a promise.** Sichuan's iron cash; private jiaozi; the Jiaozi Bureau
   of 1023 and the state issue of 1024; notes valid for fixed terms against a reserve. Meanwhile copper cash
   output passed six billion coins a year by the 1080s, on the order of ten times Tang levels, and still
   could not keep up. That is the point: paper appears where coin is heaviest and scarcest, not where the
   state is cleverest. Tie to the wealth thread and to invention-of-money's mechanism of verification moved
   forward in time.
4. **Mechanism three, print and the examined man.** Woodblock printing, three centuries old by 1000 (Loom
   founds it at the 868 Diamond Sutra), turns commercial: canons, almanacs, medical texts, and above all
   examination aids. The exam state the Han only gestured at arrives: prefectural qualifying rounds,
   departmental and palace examinations, and anonymity procedures in which names are covered and clerks
   recopy papers so handwriting cannot be recognized. Then the complication.
5. **The named debate, in two interlocking halves.** (a) Meritocracy: Edward Kracke (1947) found that in the
   surviving jinshi lists of 1148 and 1256, 56.3 and 57.9 percent of successful candidates had no paternal
   ancestor in office for three generations, and read that as mobility. Robert Hartwell and Robert Hymes
   answered that counting three lineal paternal ancestors ignores brothers, uncles and in-laws, so the same
   figures are consistent with almost no mobility in Hymes's sense; his Fuzhou study describes a localist
   turn in which elite families secured position locally rather than nationally. (b) The plateau: Needham's question; Elvin's high-level equilibrium trap, in
   which an economy this productive and this labor-rich has too little pressure to mechanize; Pomeranz's
   case that the divergence is late and turns on coal and colonies; and Broadberry, Guan and Li's national
   accounts, which put Northern Song China at the world's highest living standards with Italy passing it by
   about 1300. Present it as live. "Why did China fail" is a question about the asker; a Song official would
   not have recognized the premise.
6. **Consequence, and the thing that actually broke.** No slow senescence: in 1127 Jurchen armies took
   Kaifeng, the court fled south, and the Southern Song rebuilt at Lin'an (Hangzhou) with less territory,
   the northern iron belt lost, a larger navy, heavier reliance on maritime customs, and in time paper money
   that inflated. A plateau is a shape drawn on a graph afterward. What people lived through was a conquest.
7. **Inheritance.** Fiat money, credentialing by examination, and coal plus iron as a recipe for growth are
   all in the room by 1100, assembled by a society for its own reasons and not into an industrial revolution.
   Land the last paragraph back on the substitute: the note that is only a promise, the copy that stands in
   for the hand, the black stone that stands in for the forest. Do not end on a moral about decline.

## Factual traps

1. **Trap:** Song China produced 125,000 tons of iron in 1078, more than all Europe, full stop. **Truth:**
   that is Hartwell's reconstruction, not a recorded output. The surviving 1078 figures total about 3,300
   tonnes of government receipts, and Wagner shows both of Hartwell's key assumptions are unsupported,
   leaving the order of magnitude plausible and the calculation impossible. Attach Hartwell's name to the
   number and Wagner's objection in the same breath. **Source:** Wagner, JESHO 44.2 (2001); open preprint at
   donwagner.dk.
2. **Trap:** a Song census counted 100 million people. **Truth:** Song registers counted households and
   ding, taxable males roughly twenty to fifty-nine. The 1100 register shows 19,960,812 households and
   44,914,991 kou, about 2.25 per household, which is not a family. Modern totals multiply households by an
   assumed size; Deng uses 5.77 and gets about 115 million for 1100 and 120 million for 1110. Write
   "historians reconstruct," never "the census counted." **Source:** Deng, LSE Working Paper 76/03 (2003),
   tables 7 and 9.
3. **Trap:** Kaifeng was a city of a million. **Truth:** an estimate with a wide spread. Columbia's Asia for
   Educators gives about a million each for Kaifeng and Hangzhou; published estimates for eleventh-century
   Kaifeng run from roughly 600,000 to about 1.5 million depending on whether garrison, suburbs and
   unregistered residents are counted. Give a range and attribute, or prefer the concrete: fourteen fire
   stations and about two thousand soldiers on fire duty measure density more honestly than a headcount.
   **Source:** Asia for Educators, Columbia University, "A New Kind of City Emerges."
4. **Trap:** Zhenzong introduced Champa rice in 1012 and harvests doubled. **Truth:** early-ripening rice
   associated with Champa was already grown in Fujian before the state acted. After drought on the lower
   Yangtze and Huai, the court had on the order of thirty thousand units of seed brought from Fujian and
   distributed in the affected circuits with printed cultivation instructions; sources give 1011 or 1012, so
   state one and note the variance. Ho Ping-ti's 1956 argument that early-ripening varieties drove a medieval
   agricultural revolution is a thesis, not a measurement, and double cropping spread regionally over
   centuries. Match Loom's `rice-and-the-yangtze`. **Source:** Ho, DOI 10.2307/2591742.
5. **Trap:** Bi Sheng invented printing, and movable type transformed Song books. **Truth:** East Asian
   printing is centuries older; Bi Sheng's baked-clay type belongs to the Qingli era (1041 to 1048) and is
   known from a single passage in Shen Kuo's Mengxi bitan of about 1088, written by a man who was about
   fourteen when Bi Sheng worked. Woodblock stayed dominant for centuries, for good reasons of character
   count and reprint economics: the Song print economy is a woodblock economy. **Source:** the Mengxi bitan
   passage; confirm against a scholarly edition before quoting.
6. **Trap:** the Song government invented paper money. **Truth:** private Sichuan houses issued redeemable
   notes first, against deposits, because the regional currency was iron cash, and some failed; the state
   took the system over in 1023 and issued in 1024. The defensible superlative is "the world's first
   government-issued paper money," said once. Tang flying cash was a transfer draft, not currency, and no
   Northern Song jiaozi survives. **Source:** Asia for Educators, "Money and Credit"; cross-check von Glahn.
7. **Trap:** Song ironmasters were coking coal, running Coalbrookdale seven centuries early. **Truth:** coal
   in smelting is well evidenced from the eleventh century (Su Shi's poem; sulphur levels; the impossibly old
   radiocarbon dates). Coke specifically is a stronger claim on thinner evidence, and Wagner treats the
   charcoal-to-coal transition problem as unsolved. Write coal, state the uncertainty once, stage no
   proto-industrial revolution. **Source:** Wagner, Historical Metallurgy 37.1 (2003), 25 to 37.
8. **Trap:** the examinations made the Song a meritocracy where a farmer's son rose on merit alone.
   **Truth:** the anonymity procedures were real and remarkable, and Kracke's percentages are real, but
   Hartwell and Hymes showed the measure ignores collateral and affinal kin; the yin protection privilege
   kept placing officials' relatives in office; merchants were restricted and women were on no list at all.
   Present it as the live debate it is. **Source:** Chaffee, The Thorny Gates of Learning in Sung China
   (SUNY, new ed. 1995); Kracke, HJAS (1947); Hymes, Statesmen and Gentlemen.
9. **Three traps to verify before use, unverified in this brief.** Su Song's clock tower is dated variously
   to 1088, 1090, 1092 and 1094 and the Xinyi Xiangfayao to 1092 or 1094, so pick one chronology and cite it.
   Shen Kuo describes the magnetized needle and magnetic declination around 1088, but the earliest account of
   its use at sea is usually given as Zhu Yu's Pingzhou Ketan of 1119. And the Song was not unmilitary: it
   kept an enormous standing army that consumed most of its revenue, which is one large reason the iron
   mattered and the fiscal quarrels were bitter. Do not put a number on the army without a source.

## Callback design (ranked; use two)

1. **`invention-of-money`.** That lesson's mechanism is verification compression: a mark and a weight
   standard move some checking before the transaction, while trust still rests on standards, enforcement and
   expected future acceptance, and its last paragraph reaches explicitly toward banknotes. The jiaozi is
   that argument at the limit: the object is worth nothing, it is redeemable for iron cash nobody wants to
   carry, it circulates for a fixed term, and everything that makes it work is institutional. **Angle:**
   what did the electrum piece still have that a jiaozi note does not, and what must be true of a state
   before a stranger takes its paper? Make the answer reserves, terms, redemption and expectation, not the
   word "trust."
2. **`han-mandate`.** It ends on the point that Han recruitment was a forerunner, that a forerunner
   announces something not yet arrived, and that the Tang and Song examination state was a new machine
   claiming an old ancestor. This lesson is the arrival, so the callback must do more than say so.
   **Angle:** the Han court cut one door fifty men wide and still filled its offices by recommendation,
   clerkship, summons, rank and wealth; the Song covered names and had clerks recopy the papers. What does
   that procedure remove, and what can it not touch? Kracke, Hartwell and Hymes stand behind the answer.
3. **`rice-and-the-yangtze`.** Its image is Nuo running an unwitting annual examination on a wild grass, a
   selection too slow for any life to see. **Angle:** eight thousand years later the same crop changes
   because a court ships seed from Fujian with printed instructions. What kind of change is each, how fast,
   and why does the instruction sheet matter as much as the seed? Lets you retire the "doubled harvests"
   myth inside a question.
4. **`qin-hammer`** (alternate). Qin's bronze measure made subjects legible to the state; the Song note asks
   the reverse, that the subject find the state legible enough to accept its promise. **Angle:** which way
   does trust run in each object, and what does each cost the person holding it?

Do not call back to `tang-golden-network` even though it is eligible: its lesson is being written in parallel
and owns the maturing of the examination system. For `threadsOut`, mirror the graph plus one earned backward
thread; `invention-of-money` or `diamond-sutra` is the natural fourth.

## Node-data flags

Nothing is fabricated, but four claims are stated more confidently than the scholarship supports. Do not
edit the file; these are for Codex.

1. `summary`: "iron output unmatched until industrial Britain" states Hartwell's contested reconstruction as
   fact. Possible repair: "iron output that one classic estimate puts above any European figure before
   industrial Britain."
2. `summary`: "champa rice doubling harvests" overstates. The mechanism is earlier ripening, expansion onto
   drier and higher ground, and, regionally and slowly, double cropping. The incoming edge from
   `rice-and-the-yangtze` is correctly hedged, so summary and edge currently disagree inside the same graph.
3. `summary`: "exams staffing a meritocratic state" adopts one side of a fifty-year debate and passes over
   the yin protection privilege entirely.
4. `hook`: "cities of a million" is defensible only as a wide-ranged estimate, and "China arrives at the
   edge of the modern" is exactly the teleology the summary's last sentence then asks the reader to
   distrust. Hook and summary pull opposite ways; the lesson should side with the summary.

Two further notes. The node date, c. 1000 CE, sits about eighty years before the lesson's best material
(1024 to 1092); nothing is wrong, but Codex may prefer "c. 1000 to 1100 CE." And the edge to
`great-convergence` ("China resumes its place as the world's workshop") smuggles in a continuity the prose
should not endorse: fine as a graph echo, do not restate it as history in a `threadsOut` why.
