# Reconnaissance packet: garden-and-stoa

## Node contract

- Title: The Garden and the Stoa.
- Date in seed: c. 300 BCE. Sort: -300. Region: Europe. Thread: ideas.
- Hook facts to preserve: Epicurus teaches in a garden that death is nothing to us; Zeno teaches in a painted colonnade that virtue is the only good; philosophy is presented as therapy after Alexander.
- Seed summary: Epicurean physics explains the world through atoms and void, denies divine intervention in ordinary life, and makes freedom from fear and pain the relevant pleasure. Stoicism teaches providential cosmos, character as the only true good, and emotions as judgments that can be retrained. Both schools were rival manuals for living and later influenced Rome.
- Outgoing targets: `jesus-to-constantine` and `rebirth-of-the-ancients`.
- Edge logic: later Christian debate and adaptation, then Renaissance recovery of Lucretius. Do not say Christians or Renaissance humanists simply adopted either school wholesale.

## Chronology

- Epicurus lived 341 to 270 BCE and founded his school in Athens around 306 BCE. Zeno of Citium lived c. 334 to c. 262 BCE and began teaching in Athens around the early third century BCE.
- Alexander died in 323 BCE. The schools emerged in the successor-world after his conquests, not during one shared moment with him.
- The “Garden” was Epicurus' school and community; the “Stoa” was named for the Painted Stoa where Zeno taught. Avoid treating either as a building preserved unchanged from the founders.
- Lucretius lived in the first century BCE, centuries after Epicurus. Cicero and Seneca are later Roman mediators, not founders.
- Christian writers and Renaissance readers are later reception histories. The 1417 recovery of Lucretius belongs to the fifteenth century CE, not the ancient node.

## Debates and traps

- “Epicurean” does not mean lavish consumption. The target is stable pleasure, freedom from disturbance, modest desires, friendship, and release from fear.
- “Stoic” does not mean emotionless. Stoic theory analyzes passions as judgments and seeks rationally ordered response, not blank numbness.
- Do not present atomism as modern physics. Epicurean atoms, void, soul, and swerve belong to an ancient philosophical system.
- Do not imply the schools were founded because Greek people suddenly lost religion after Alexander. They offered competing therapies within a religious and political world.
- The founders' writings survive unevenly. Epicurus' letters are mediated by later transmission; most Stoic founder texts are fragmentary.
- Avoid a simple binary in which Epicureans are private and Stoics are political. Both traditions had broader interventions and later Roman variations.
- The graph's “both agreed philosophy cures misery” is a synthesis, not a direct quotation. Cite each school's own therapeutic aim separately.

## Scene evidence

- Athens after Alexander and the successor kingdoms as the political background, with caution against claiming universal trauma or one social response.
- Epicurean garden community, friendship, simple food, letters, and the distinction among natural, necessary, natural but unnecessary, and empty desires.
- Painted Stoa, public teaching, Zeno's arguments about living according to nature, virtue, assent, and judgments.
- Primary textual objects: Epicurus' Letter to Menoeceus; later reports in Diogenes Laertius; fragments and Roman mediations.
- Herculaneum papyri and Lucretius are later physical evidence for Epicurean transmission, not objects in Epicurus' original garden.
