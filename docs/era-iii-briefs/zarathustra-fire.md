# Reconnaissance packet: zarathustra-fire

## Node contract

- Graph node: **Zarathustra’s Fire**, c. 1200 to 1000 BCE, `sasia`, ideas.
- Seed mechanism: an eastern Iranian or Central Asian priest-poet uses hymn to frame Ahura Mazda, truth (`aša`) and the lie as a field of human choice.
- Outgoing targets: `israel-one-god` via debated Persian-period and exilic contact; `crown-of-cyrus` via debated continuity between Gathic teaching and Achaemenid royal religion.
- Seed conflict: the Gathas are seventeen Old Avestan hymns, but Zarathustra’s date, homeland, authorship of every hymn, and relation to later Zoroastrian doctrine remain disputed. Do not equate the Gathic moral contrast with the fully developed later dualist system without qualification.

## Chronology

- The graph’s c. 1200 to 1000 BCE is a working placement, not a secure biography. Iranica gives a broad scholarly range from the mid-second millennium to the early sixth century BCE, with a rough consensus around c. 1000 BCE.
- The Gathas predate the Achaemenid kings by several centuries if the early dating is right. Cyrus ruled 549 to 530 BCE; Darius ruled 522 to 486 BCE. Do not call Zarathustra and Darius contemporaries.
- The traditional “258 years before Alexander” date is a late chronological construction tied to Zoroastrian cosmic chronology, not an independently dated event.
- Achaemenid inscriptions naming Ahuramazda are sixth to fourth century BCE evidence for royal use of the name, not direct proof that Achaemenid practice reproduced the Gathas.
- The Gathas are Old Avestan and linguistically close to early Vedic Sanskrit; linguistic affinity does not establish a shared date, location, or direct borrowing.

## Debates and traps

- “Zoroastrianism” is an umbrella for a long historical development. Keep Gathic evidence, Younger Avesta, Achaemenid inscriptions, Sasanian theology, and modern practice in separate layers.
- The Gathic opposition between `aša` and `druj` is not automatically the later Ahura Mazda versus Angra Mainyu cosmology in its fully systematized form.
- Do not repeat popular claims that Zoroastrianism invented Jewish monotheism, heaven, hell, Satan, or apocalypse. Persian-period contact is plausible in some cases, but direction, mechanism, and dating are debated.
- Do not call Zarathustra “the first monotheist” without explaining the classification problem and the Gathas’ complex divine vocabulary.
- “Fire worshipper” is a hostile or flattening label. Fire is a ritual and theological medium; the Gathic evidence is not a simple doctrine that fire itself is the supreme god.
- A biographical scene must be composite or text-bound. No securely dated archaeological setting identifies Zarathustra’s household, patron, fire altar, or conversion.

## Scene evidence

- Real text object: a short, metrical Old Avestan hymn corpus embedded in the Yasna liturgy; seventeen songs, five metrical groups.
- Real ritual object: the Ašəm Vohū prayer, transmitted at Yasna 27.14, immediately before the Gathas; translation varies because grammar and vocabulary are difficult.
- Textual setting: oral performance and later manuscript transmission; the surviving manuscripts are not contemporary with the proposed second-millennium composition.
- Social vocabulary: household, district, herds, patrons, priests, rivals, and the figure of Vištāspa appear in or around the Gathic evidence, but the narrative identity of each person must remain cautious.
- Sensory evidence available to a writer: metrical recitation, repeated formula, animal wealth, sacrificial language, and a fire-centered ritual environment. Treat these as evidence categories, not as recovered eyewitness detail.
- Useful human figure: Zarathustra as the first-person voice of selected hymns, while flagging the scholarly debate over whether all Gathic first-person passages are autobiographical.

## Eligible callbacks

- `rigveda`: both are Old Indo-Iranian poetic corpora with metrical, oral, priestly transmission; comparison must not imply direct borrowing or identical theology.
- `oldest-song`: both make song a technology for preserving collective memory, but the Gathas are a liturgical corpus and the earlier lesson concerns a different archaeological and linguistic context.
- `invention-of-writing`: contrast a sacred tradition maintained through oral precision with writing’s later ability to stabilize, classify, and transport claims.
- `epic-of-gilgamesh`: compare a text recovered through later clay-tablet copying with hymns whose early life was oral and whose manuscript evidence is much later.
