Claims deliberately softened or omitted:

Source verifications and replacements:
- None. All three deeper objects are the handoff's recommended candidates, each opened and verified: the Attalus page confirms J. B. Explicit reconstructions:

Adversarial review findings (applied):
- [should] The story misassigns the fire-equipment order to Trajan and treats it as uncertain, when Pliny's own letter documents that he had already ordered the equipment before writing. The lesson instead builds the sequence as Trajan deciding 'Better... to supply the fire-fighting apparatus itself... Nicomedia would get equipment' and closes with 'The pumps and buckets, presumably, arrived,' hedging something whose ordering is attested and implying the buckets waited on Rome. The handoff's Preferred scene section explicitly states 'By the time he wrote... he had already ordered equipment supplied.' The documented sequence is also better history for this lesson's thesis: the governor equipped the city on his own authority and consulted Rome only about the association.

Remaining nits (left for your judgment):
- The Res Gestae paraphrase 'he exceeded all men afterward only in influence, holding no more office than his colleagues' renders potestas as 'office,' which is both imprecise and near-tautological (colleagues in a magistracy by definition hold the same office). RG 34 claims he had no more official power (potestas) than his colleagues in each magistracy, while exceeding all in auctoritas.

## teotihuacan-maya

- Words (story plus significance): 1794
- Single-file check: OK: 365 nodes, 799 wires, 10 eras, 1 lessons. 0 warning(s).
- Review verdict: revise (revised, recheck OK)

Claims deliberately softened or omitted:
- The 378 CE episode is presented as an arrival with a same-day royal death, not a conquest; David Stuart's conquest argument is named as one reading beside coup and alliance readings, and the mechanism is called one of Maya archaeology's sharpest open questions.
- No to-the-day CE conversion is given for the 378 arrival (it depends on the correlation constant); the prose says January of 378 CE and lets the Maya calendar's own day-precision carry the exactness.
- Population is capped at 'perhaps one hundred thousand' and labeled an estimate from dwelling space rather than a census; the only superlative is 'largest city yet built in the Americas' (Cowgill's framing), never a world ranking.
- Teotihuacan is never called kingless, egalitarian, or an empire: monarchy, constrained rulership, and more collective governance are kept as live hypotheses per Cowgill 1997, and the Feathered Serpent Pyramid sacrifices anchor the claim that coercive power existed regardless.
- The city is not called writingless or literate: Langley's roughly 120-sign notation is described with the encode-speech debate left explicitly open, and the Met fragment's logogram reading is given as 'may.'
- Maya script is 'best understood of the pre-Columbian Americas,' not the only one: Zapotec signs noted as older, other systems as unread; no astronomy superlatives anywhere.
- Teotihuacan's murals are not called public art: the lesson notes they decorated interior compound rooms and that audiences are unknown.
- The two endings are kept apart: c. 550 CE selective burning of Teotihuacan's center versus the staggered eighth-to-ninth-century southern Maya dynastic transformations, with living Mayan-language communities named.
- Stela 31's side portraits are described by costume ('a warrior of the west') rather than asserted as proof of Teotihuacan presence; the Teotihuacan link is carried by obsidian, architecture, and the stranger's multi-city name spread, all sourced.
- The 'boy king' commonplace for Yax Nuun Ahiin's age at accession was dropped as not needed and not independently verified; he is 'the son of a foreign lord.'

Source verifications and replacements:

- The old woman is wholly composite: her rite, inherited censer, forty years of mornings, roof view, and hearing several languages in one morning are invented, though every setting element (courtyards, drains, subfloor burials, household censers, the deep-red murals, the flowering trees with signs, the clawed flowery-speech being) is attested at Teotihuacan or in the Met's Techinantitla record.
